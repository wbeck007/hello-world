THE QUESTION BEHIND MY ARTIFACT

Not just what we invented.
What it let us do.

Reading The Coming Wave for IDH3034 — Humans in the Age of Bots led me to a paradox: a technology used to reproduce sacred texts could also circulate arguments against established authority. My interest is in what happened after the invention—how people used it to learn, question, organize, and eventually share something as trivial as a cat meme.

I think of this as opening Pandora’s box: once a technology enters society, people discover uses its creators never anticipated. The story that interests me is how those possibilities reshape communication, culture, and work.

01 / SACRED WORDSMAINZ · c. 1455

A workshop.
A Bible.
A new scale.

Gutenberg’s Bible put an established religious text at the center of a major change in European book production. Movable metal type brought materials, machinery, and skilled hands together to reproduce texts on a new scale.

What became possible: people could reproduce the same text in multiple copies without handwriting each entire copy. Ideas could travel in a repeatable form, giving readers in different places a shared text to encounter and discuss.

The Bible combined repeatable printing with hand-colored headings and capitals. The press and the craftsperson worked together.

Who could publish? +

A new way to put words into circulation.

Gutenberg’s Latin Bible brought technical invention and skilled bookmaking together. A text could be reproduced for different readers while craftspeople added decoration by hand. This changed the work of making books and expanded the possibilities for circulating knowledge.

02 / AUTHORITY QUESTIONEDEUROPE · 1517

The same press.
A different message.

The technology that reproduced religious texts could also spread disputes about religious authority. Luther’s Ninety-Five Theses began as an academic challenge in 1517; printing helped carry the controversy beyond its initial setting.

What became possible: people could encounter, reproduce, and debate a challenge to authority across different places. The audience for a disagreement no longer had to fit inside the room where it began.

How did print unsettle authority? +

A use no single inventor could prescribe.

The Library of Congress describes printing as crucial to the rapid spread of Luther’s challenge. Public interest in the controversy also gave printers a market for these works. Commercial demand and religious debate could reinforce one another.

This is the paradox that fascinates me: the same means of circulating sacred texts also helped circulate challenges to religious authority. Printers, writers, and readers turned a tool of reproduction into a medium for public argument.

Later, Paine’s Common Sense (1776) used the pamphlet form to argue for American independence. Reprinting let an argument travel through different communities.

My question: what happens in modern digital spaces where people exist in echo chambers that reinforce their already-held ideas?

03 / IDEAS BECOME MOVEMENTSLONDON · 1848

A shared text.
A call to arms.

Print could give dispersed readers a common vocabulary for their working lives. In 1848, Marx and Engels published The Communist Manifesto, a political programme commissioned by the Communist League. Labour became the subject of an argument intended to circulate beyond its authors.

What became possible: people in different places could discuss the same account of class and work, circulate its demands, and use it as a reference for political organization. A shared programme gave organizers a text to circulate, discuss, and put into practice.

How does an idea become collective? +

A common reference across distance.

In their 1872 preface, Marx and Engels describe the League’s commission and the Manifesto’s publication and translations. Translation and republication helped these arguments reach readers across linguistic and national boundaries.

The short political programme of the Manifesto (1848) and the sustained economic analysis of Capital, Volume I (1867), show how different forms of writing could bring ideas about labour to different audiences.

Workers also published their own literary voices. The Lowell Offering included poetry and fiction by women textile workers; the National Park Service preserves a March 1845 cover. Print gave space to workers’ experiences, imagination, and political aspirations.

Labour lens: can shared language help people recognize a common problem—and act together?

04 / THE OPEN WEBCERN · 30 APRIL 1993

Publish a page.
Share a link.

The web began as a way for scientists to share information. In 1993, CERN put its World Wide Web software in the public domain. Publishing could increasingly happen through connected computers, without producing a physical copy for each reader.

What became possible: a connected person could read a page, link others to it, publish a response, or seek people with similar experiences. Communication increasingly offered a two-way avenue: readers could also become contributors.

How did communication become two-way? +

The reader joins the conversation.

Invented at CERN in 1989, the web gained an important opening on 30 April 1993 when CERN released its software into the public domain. People could build on a shared foundation for linking and exchanging information.

For me, the important change is the two-way avenue. We can seek information, respond to it, and send our own ideas back into circulation. Behind these everyday exchanges are the people who build and maintain the software and networks.

Labour lens: when distribution becomes digital, which work disappears from view?

05 / THE EVERYDAY POSTCONNECTED LIFE · TODAY

And now,
this cat.

A message no longer needs the weight of a Bible or a political argument to be shared. An ordinary person can post a joke to an online community with a potentially worldwide audience. The trivial becomes publishable too.

What became possible: ordinary users could circulate jokes, opinions, and everyday experiences without persuading a print publisher to carry them. Each reply or repost could make a consumer part of the distribution network.

A closer look at the online conversation.

In Pew Research Center's 2021 analysis of U.S. adult Twitter users, the most active 25% produced 97% of tweets during the study period, June 12–September 12.

FROM A SCARCE TEXT TO AN ENDLESS FEED

We read.
We reply.
We circulate.

For me, the cat meme makes a serious point with some levity. The ability to circulate information has become ordinary enough to feel unremarkable. Yet every post, comment, and share places us inside the process by which ideas move.

My concern now is abundance: notifications, headlines, and opinions can make access to information feel like an endless stream. We consume this stream, but we also help create it: every response, opinion, and repost adds to the circulation of ideas.

What changes when AI joins the conversation? +

The next Pandora’s box question.

In my blog, I ask what happens when information comes through a tailored conversation with an AI. Does that help us understand more, or give a new intermediary greater influence over what we encounter? This is the next question I want my artifact to explore.

Making this artifact gave that question a practical dimension. I tried learning interactive HTML through online tutorials, then collaborated with language models to develop and refine the site. Natural-language instructions became part of how I could build something for others to use.

That experience connects directly to labour: which tasks become easier, which skills remain necessary, and how does responsibility shift? Reading developers’ accounts of working with AI made these questions feel immediate. Building this page let me experience that shift for myself.

The thread running through my artifact: what does a technology enable people to do—and what follows that its creators did not intend?

Historical context & data notes +

Printing and earlier movable-type techniques existed in Asia before Gutenberg. This story begins with his European workshop as one influential example. Manuscripts, teaching, letters, and oral communication also circulated ideas before the press. The Reformation illustrates printing’s role in a wider social change; printing was one contributor alongside other forces. The Manifesto appeared in 1848; Capital, Volume I, followed in 1867. These are distinct works with different purposes. The ITU figures measure internet use, not the number of active publishers or people heard. The 1994 bar shows an upper bound of 1%. Pew’s figures concern posting volume among U.S. adult Twitter users during June 12–September 12, 2021, rather than Reddit or global participation. These measurements describe different aspects of participation and should not be treated as a single historical count of voices.
